Non-Quality Cost from
Duplicate Customer Records

A quantitative framework for estimating annual financial impact of duplicate records on CLV accuracy, servicing quality, and cross-sell targeting in an insurance book of business.

01 — Master formula

Total Non-Quality Cost

The total cost decomposes into three independent components, each representing a distinct failure mode caused by the inability to identify a unique customer across fragmented records.

NQC (annual estimate)
NQC  =  C₁  +  C₂ₐ  +  C₂ᵦ
C₁
Wrong servicing cost — churn-driven CLV loss
High-value customers misclassified into a lower segment receive an inferior service level, increasing their probability of churning.
C₁ = Nᵤ · φ · πHV · P↓ · ΔΛ · CLVHV
NᵤUnique customers — total distinct customers in the book
φDuplicate prevalence — share of customers with ≥1 duplicate record
πHVHigh-value share — proportion in the top CLV segment
P↓Segment downgrade prob. — probability a duplicate-affected HV customer is misclassified
ΔΛIncremental churn rate — additional annual churn attributable to wrong service level
CLVHVHigh-value CLV — net present value of expected lifetime value per HV customer
C₂ₐ
Missed x-sell revenue — foregone margin
A fragmented customer profile causes the scoring model to exclude HV customers from x-sell campaigns, missing conversion opportunities.
C₂ₐ = Nᵤ · φ · πHV · Pmiss · CRHV · Mxs
PmissX-sell miss prob. — probability a duplicate-affected HV customer falls below the targeting threshold
CRHVX-sell conversion rate — expected conversion on HV-targeted cross-sell offers
MxsX-sell margin — gross margin per successful cross-sell product placed
C₂ᵦ
Wasted campaign spend — redundant contact cost
Duplicate records trigger multiple outreach touches to the same individual. The surplus contacts beyond the first are pure waste.
C₂ᵦ = Nᵤ · φ · (d̄ − 1) · Cxc
d̄Avg duplicate records — mean total records per duplicate-affected customer
CxcContact cost — fully-loaded cost per x-sell outreach attempt (digital, mail, phone)

03 — Calibration guidance

How to estimate parameters

The formula is only as good as its inputs. Here is where each parameter should come from in practice.

P↓ and Pmiss
The hardest parameters to estimate without a data exercise. Run a matching sample: score CLV on fragmented records versus merged records and observe how many HV customers fall below segment or targeting thresholds. Even a 1,000-record sample will give a defensible estimate.
ΔΛ — Incremental churn
Should come from a churn model that controls for service tier. If an experimental holdout exists where service tier was misassigned, use observed churn directly. Do not assume — even 1% incremental churn at scale moves NQC materially.
CLVHV — Lifetime value
Use net present value of future premiums minus expected claims and administration costs — not gross written premium. Using revenue inflates C₁ significantly. A 5-year horizon discounted at the firm's cost of capital is a reasonable default.
Overlap correction
C₁ and C₂ₐ are not fully independent: a customer excluded from x-sell targeting who also churns is double-counted. Apply a correction of (1 − ρ) to C₂ₐ where ρ is the correlation between the two failure modes. In most books ρ is small enough to ignore in the first pass.
φ — Duplicate prevalence
Run a deterministic + probabilistic matching exercise on a stratified sample (name, DoB, postcode, policy number) to get a defensible prevalence rate. Industry benchmarks for insurance range from 8% to 25% depending on legacy system history.
Using the model for ROI
NQC is the upper bound of value available through deduplication. A data remediation programme costing less than NQC has a positive expected return. Use C₂ᵦ alone as the easiest, most defensible floor — it requires no CLV assumptions.
